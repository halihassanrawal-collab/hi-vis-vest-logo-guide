Heat Transfer, Screen Print or Embroidery? Putting Logos on Hi-Vis Vests

A hi-vis vest is one of the cheapest and most visible places to put your company name. It is seen by clients, the public and other contractors every day. But vests are made from thin, often mesh-like polyester, and that limits how logos can be applied. Choosing the right decoration method for custom hi vis vests affects how good the logo looks, how long it lasts and whether the vest still meets the visibility standard it was made to.

Short answer: Heat transfers are the most common choice for hi-vis vests because they suit thin polyester and handle fine detail. Screen printing works well for large, simple designs on bigger orders. Embroidery is possible on solid-fabric vests but is heavier and less common on mesh.

Why do vests need special care with logos?

Hi-vis vests rely on fluorescent background fabric and reflective tape to make the wearer visible. Standards such as EN ISO 20471 and ANSI/ISEA 107 set minimum areas for both. A logo printed over reflective tape or covering too much fluorescent fabric can reduce visibility. Keep logos in the areas the manufacturer recommends, usually the left chest and the upper back panel, and never across the tape.

When are heat transfers the best option?

Heat transfers are printed onto a carrier film and pressed onto the vest with heat and pressure. They bond well to polyester, can reproduce small text and multiple colours, and do not need the setup costs of screens. That makes them a practical choice for small to medium orders and for logos with fine detail. Ask the supplier to use a transfer designed for polyester and to confirm the wash performance they expect.

When does screen printing make sense?

Screen printing pushes ink through a stencil onto the fabric. It is efficient for large orders with simple one- or two-colour designs, such as a company name across the back. Each colour needs its own screen, so setup costs are higher, but the unit cost falls as quantity rises. On open mesh vests, ink can sit less evenly, so ask to see a sample on the exact fabric before you commit.

Is embroidery suitable for vests?

Embroidery gives a premium, durable look, but it adds weight and stiffness. On lightweight mesh vests, stitches can pull the fabric. On solid-fabric vests, especially heavier ones such as surveyor or executive styles, embroidery can work well on the chest. It is usually not used for large back designs on vests.

How do you choose between them?

Consider order size, logo complexity, fabric type and how often vests are washed. For most crews, a heat-transferred chest logo and a printed back panel strike a good balance. For a premium vest worn by managers, embroidery may be worth considering. In every case, approve a physical sample before bulk production.

Frequently asked questions

Will a logo affect the vest's compliance?

It can if it covers reflective tape or too much fluorescent fabric. Keep logos in approved areas and ask your supplier to confirm placement.

How long do printed logos last on vests?

That depends on the method, the vest fabric and the washing routine. Follow the care label and ask your supplier about expected wash performance.

Can we add names or role titles?

Yes. Heat transfers make it easy to personalise vests with names or roles such as "Supervisor" or "First Aider".

Key takeaways

  • Heat transfers suit most hi-vis vests and fine logos.
  • Screen printing is efficient for large, simple orders.
  • Embroidery works best on solid-fabric vests, not mesh.
  • Never place logos over reflective tape.

Final thoughts

The right logo method keeps your vests looking professional without undermining the reason people wear them. Think about your order size, artwork and fabric, and always check a sample.
